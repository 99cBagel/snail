## Case 1 — The Yellow Bracelet Project, China (2012–2024)

**Client:** Eisai Co. Ltd (China)

**Tagline:** Patient education · AD awareness · NGO campaign design · national, decade-long campaign · branded wearable

- **Situation:** Eisai Co. Ltd. China wanted to expand the market position of Aricept, the leader in its class — indicated for cognitive function, and taken by the public as the Alzheimer's / dementia pill. China had no patient-advocacy success story.
- **Pain Points:**
- Eisai: wanted to push direct-to-consumer in China, but local regulation prohibits it — no direct-to-consumer avenue.
- The NGO: most NGOs lacked the resources and the experience to run such a campaign, and most campaigns carried no brand-emblazoned keepsake — they settled down to a news release and a photo opportunity. The NGO was in dire need of a recognizable brand wearable.
- At-risk families: families needed a free, effective way to protect parents or elderly who wander and get lost, and a way to stay updated with education and prevention.
- Chinese society: Alzheimer's disease stigmatism is pervasive.
- **Actions:** My company, Presenter, came in with the Yellow Bracelet Project — an Eisai–NGO–Presenter three-party collaboration. Eisai footed the cost, the NGO distributed the bracelet, and Presenter took care of the backend database. The core is a QR-code-bearing bracelet: a wearable, scannable tag, given free to "at-risk" families — families who may have parents or elderly at risk of wandering and getting lost. To register the bracelet, a family member needed to subscribe to a weekly podcast on the WeChat platform. In 2012 WeChat was at its burgeoning, booming start; many were starting to use WeChat as a browser and article reader. I held the project from 2012. In 2020 I had to leave Beijing because of COVID, and between 2020 and 2024 I managed the project remotely from the US. The project was then terminated: after the new drug lecanemab came out in China, Eisai removed resources from this project to focus on the new product launch — and without financial backing, the project terminated.
- **Result:**
- For Eisai: there was no direct-to-consumer avenue; with this project they embedded the product pitch into patient-awareness articles — soft-pitching. Every year, their soft pitch got exposure of 100 articles × 500,000 audience. The audience is highly focused and matches their target.
- For the NGO: having the bracelet kept the campaign going for over a decade. It is a low-maintenance, well-respected project, and the campaign serves as one of the most recognizable brands among all NGOs in China.
- For at-risk families: the bracelet is free and effective, and families are kept updated with education and prevention.
- For Chinese society: where Alzheimer's disease stigmatism is pervasive, the project helped alleviate stigmatisms.
- The project was quoted as a guidance example in *Dementia Friendly Communities* (2017, global edition). To this date, over 500,000 bands have been given out free; every year, thousands of wandering-lost would get help, and millions of hits would fall on the web site.
- **What made it hard:** Direct-to-consumer promotion was prohibited by regulation, so the pitch had to travel inside patient education. NGOs lacked the resources and experience to run a campaign of this kind. And Alzheimer's stigma in China was pervasive — the campaign had to win trust before it could help.
- **What may be shared, and how anonymized:** names and info are available to share (per Part 1).
- **Postscript:** Eisai has switched resources from Aricept to Leqembi (lecanemab), which received approval to market in China in 2024.

Review coverage — from *Dementia Friendly Communities* developments (global edition), https://www.alzint.org/u/dfc-developments.pdf :

> The campaign Memory Health in the Community was initiated in 2010, aiming to improve the knowledge, recognition and prevention of dementia in the community. Memory specialists, social workers and community staff gave lectures to raise awareness and support dementia screening in more than 50 cities. Dementia screening is now accessible in more than 500 communities in China.
> >
> In 2012, the Yellow Bracelet Project was initiated to encourage safety and prevent people with dementia getting lost. Yellow Bracelet has now become a symbol of affection, and continues to attract attention across society. By the end of 2016, the project had distributed 500,000 free yellow bracelets in 233 cities. The Safe Bracelet Project followed in 2016 in Wenzhou. With the support of a GPS-guided bracelet, 84 people with dementia have been found after they wandered to distant areas.

## Case 2 — Lunch Learn and SMS Feedback (LL-SMS), China (2005–2012)

**Client:** Pfizer, Novartis, AstraZeneca, Sanofi, Roche, Medtronics, Boston Sci, NovoN, and many more

**Tagline:** Detailing  · Lunch Learn and SMS(短信科室会) · KOL video on CD · sales-rep call · expense report · Salesforce effectiveness

- **Situation:** China, 2005–2012: mobile data, streaming video, and unlimited data were not available; no Zoom meeting, no VPN, no virtual workspace. Cellular data was expensive, and sending photos over a mobile phone was not practical. Pharma corporations had hired fleets of sales reps spread around the country.
- **Pain Points:**
- Pharma corporations: fleets of sales reps spread around the country — Pfizer China alone employed over 8,000 sales reps, and Novartis, AstraZeneca, and Sanofi all had similar forces — but only limited tools to track their visit activities.
- Field sales reps: missions of visit counts per week, but no ability to brief on recent publications and no way to warm up a session — a knowledge gap in detailing — and no way to substantiate an expense report: the number of activities lacked evidence to build trust with the corporate accounting department.
- Doctors: limited resources for continuing education and limited access to recent publications, and no way to keep themselves updated.
- **Actions:** At Presenter, we made a novice design from a new combo of the technologies at hand — no mobile CRM software, no online CRM login, no AI to decipher recent publications. A lunch session at the site, a prerecorded KOL video, a dynamically generated session ID, and SMS sent to a designated line with the session ID:
- The sales rep used SMS to obtain a session ID.
- A prerecorded KOL video on CD — featuring the talker and PPT — was played by the rep in the lunch session; the CD ended with a quiz or poll.
- Doctors sent SMS in the format "session ID, answer ABCD".
- We summarized from all sessions and fed the results to the pharma's corporate and accounting departments.
- Behind the session sat the department-meeting interactive system (科室会互动系统): a representative database (session-code distribution and attendee archiving), a doctor database (region, city, PIM records, answer records), a question-bank module, an answering module that matched SMS answers to the session's topic automatically, a hotline trigger module, a lucky-draw module, and an analysis module that generated reports — plus the SMS platform itself, and filmed courseware production (KOL talk filmed with professional crew and studio, PPT synced with the video in post-production, and a client program with warm-up video, meeting-code activation, timed Q&A, and on-site draw).
- **Result:** The initiative was received well — so much success that nearly ALL major pharmas in Beijing and Shanghai came to Presenter to implement LL-SMS, popularly known as "短信科室会" (Lunch-Learn and SMS Session); it was at this time that the term became coined. Market adoption lasted for a few years, until the age of streaming video and mobile data, and video conference calls.

## Case 3 — Weekly Journal Abstract via MMS, China (2010–2013)

**Client:** Pfizer, Novartis, AstraZeneca, Sanofi, Roche, and Novo Nordisk

**Tagline:** Novice medical communication · KOL-written abstracts · weekly journal by MMS · 1,500 characters vs SMS 60 · literature in Chinese for practitioners

- **Situation:** Accessing an original publication was expensive, and most practitioner doctors could not work through an in-depth English article.
- **Pain Points:**
- Pharma: wanted to engage in medical communication, which has a proven success of branding a product as evidence-supported — but the access of a full article is quite expensive.
- Sales reps: wanted to be updated about new developments, but had a knowledge gap even if given the original publication.
- Physicians: wanted to be updated with the recent literature, but joining a conference is expensive; even if given the original publication, they may not be able to digest it with the English.
- **Actions:** At Presenter, we hired KOLs to read the original publications and write abstracts in Chinese, and published them weekly via long message — MMS, up to 1,500 characters, while an SMS holds only 60. Each issue carried abstracts compiled from the field's authoritative journals, a quiz at the end answered by SMS for points, and a route for readers to request the full text. The service was free to the final reader.
- **Result:** A running journal service, not a one-off campaign. The sample issue below (2011) was serving 828 hospitals and 4,695 endocrinology physicians, with 364 physicians joining the previous issue's SMS quiz.
