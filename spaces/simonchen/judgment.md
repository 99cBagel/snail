# Judgment

Status: Drafted by the assistant 2026-11-17 at the principal's request, synthesized only from his own Parts 1–3 accounts (Assessment traps, the three cases, the Remediation fixes). Approved by the principal and published 2026-11-17.

## Red flags

- A low-budget customer — the project cannot carry a large effort.
- A client contact who is making a move to another position — they usually do not have passion for a large project.
- Money flowing directly from a sole-supporter sponsor to the agency — it appears as financing direct-to-consumer advertisement, a legal risk in a time when many pharmas were fined for compliance and corruption.
- The client's powerful internal medical team must approve the wording — expect disagreement and rejection cycles on expression.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming a tech solution exists for an operations problem — for the SMS modem PCs there was none; manpower carried it.
- Letting a rollout start with bugs or hiccups — trainees who experience a poor start register complaints to the responsible people in the client's organization, and it becomes a hurdle at the start.
- Letting contracted writers face the client's medical team directly — the work needs medically trained liaisons in between to review and coordinate the reconciliation.
- Neglecting the trainee's first touch — if login and kick start are not familiar and the UI is not simple, trainee users resist.

## Decision rules

- When assessing a client, catch their pain points first, then understand the budget limit, then survey their understanding of technology, because the pitch — novice technology, new tools — only lands after those three are known.
- When a sponsor's direct money flow creates a compliance risk, reroute the flow through the NGO and earn the work through proposal and bidding, because every step must follow the laws and regulation, and the deal must be earned.
- When there is no tech solution, keep high manpower on it and motivate the engineers for overtime and extra effort, because the client's sessions must run without interruption.
- When trainees must enter a system, start with the UI design and a printed pocket-size handout for login and kick start, because no resistance from trainee users is the goal.
- When marketing clients plan by yearly budget and want to stage something different from the previous year, pitch ideas and novelty, because that is what attracts their attention.
