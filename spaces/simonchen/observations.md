# Personal Observations

Status: Part 5, drafted 2026-11-17 at the principal's direction: sharing personal observation of common problems in the field. His five questions, rephrased to three. All three questions answered by the principal 2026-11-17; approved and published 2026-11-17.

## 1. Common problems in my field

What are the common problems in my field? Which are the most urgent, and still unmet?

For cleanroom-related skill or SOP training, I often found lack of technology adoption — or the tech adoption is missing. I believe there is a lack of innovation in line operation. There seems to be deep resistance to changes. There is always lack of champion for new method. As for cleanroom QA or QMS, I believe the same slow technology adoption also persists. The current QMS or SOP process seems to be an invisible deterrent for new try. Lack of try is the very eminent in this field.

## 2. Where the lasting solutions are

From my experience, where can the lasting solutions be found?

Need to encourage culture of try. Also need to ask for leave way to try. If possible, an innovation section or methods should be included in the SOP. There is a need to make try as mandatory. And there needs to be proof, and the commitment should be audited. If fails should be treated as CAPA or with significance. Upper management should encourage such institutional tryouts.

## 3. Why others may disagree

Why might others disagree with me? And why do I believe I am right?

People may cite lack of incidents and question the economy of the tryout. If ain't broke, don't fix it. My argument is that with AI advancement, consultants and executives should address the culture aspect of running a medical or operation facility. While the adoption of AI can fasten the existing workflow, it is likely that it can also help discover improvement.
