# Playbooks

Status: Assessment, Training rollout, and Remediation approved and published 2026-11-17 (Interview Part 3). Investigation and Transfer are still to fill. Each method: the sequence, what gets looked at first, the traps.

## Assessment

- **Sequence:** Catch their pain points first. Understand the budget limit. Survey their understanding of technology. Then show my pitch — with the words like "novice technology", "new tools".
- **Look at first:** Most of my clients are marketing background in pharma. They know of my past experience, and will usually listen for novice and innovation. Marketing prepares their budget by year, and every year they like to stage something different from the previous year — ideas and novelty attract their attention.
- **Traps:** Watch out for low-budget customers. Watch out for people making a move to another position — they usually do not have passion for a large project.

## Training rollout

- **Sequence:** Prepare a printed credit-card-like pocket-size handout — it is designed for trainees to be familiar with the login and how to kick start. If the project requires trainees to enter, I would start with the UI design first.
- **Look at first:** I first make sure the UI is simple and straightforward. I want to see no resistance from the trainee users.
- **Traps:** Initial bugs or hiccups, when trainees experience a poor start. They would register complaints to the responsible people in my client's organization. This becomes a hurdle at the start.

## Remediation

Built case by case — for each case: what went bad (one or two instances), and how it was fixed.

### Case 1 — The Yellow Bracelet Project

- **What went bad:** This is a 3-party collaboration with Eisai as the sole supporter, and the money flow was from Eisai to me directly. After 2 years of the start, Eisai found itself at risk of law, for appearing to finance an agency to commit D2P (direct-to-consumer) advertisement. The concern was legit — because at that time many pharmas were fined for compliance and corruption.
- **How it was fixed:** I took time to understand the concern, and I also spent time to solicit help from the NGO party. I asked the financial flow to be rerouted in this flow: Eisai to NGO, and from NGO to me. Every step we follow the laws and regulation — NGO operation is also highly regulated in China. Presenter had to enter proposal, bidding and winning the bid process. The new flow is this: redesign the Yellow Bracelet as a bracelet-giving charity campaign. Eisai sponsors the charity to NGO, and NGO per the governing law needs to disburse the majority of donation to public, while keeping an 8% management fee. We at Presenter went through bidding to earn this disbursement. There are a few technicalities, but we learned to obey the law and earn the deal.

### Case 2 — LL-SMS (Lunch Learn and SMS Feedback)

- **What went bad:** At that time SMS was the only way of data transfer wirelessly. There was no server, no web portal. We needed to rely on serial port modems to capture the incoming SMS. The modems are attached to the serial port of a host PC, and the host PC has only up to 6 or 8 serial ports available. We had so many client projects at that time that we had to keep like 5 or 6 PCs running 24x7. We did not have a service agent to host these PCs — they were all stacked up in our office. Maintaining them was very challenging. If one failed, the remote LL-SMS session would get disrupted realtime.
- **How it was fixed:** There was no tech solution to this. We had to keep high manpower to maintain. I was able to motivate engineers for overtime and for extra effort. We got to keep the running going well, without interrupting client's sessions.

### Case 3 — Weekly Journal Abstract via MMS

- **What went bad:** The essence of this operation is abstracts of publications. Selection and abstract writing are put to the shoulder of contracted medical writers. We hired a team of moonlighters for the tasks. At the same time their works are emailed to clients for approval. Our client also passes the job to the internal medical team — every pharma has a powerful medical team. Wording and expression must pass through the scrutiny eyes of the medical dept. Disagreement and rejections often take place, causing our work to be late or close to be late.
- **How it was fixed:** I had assigned employees with medical training or background to act as a liaison. The two employees review the moonlighters' work and pass to client, and then coordinate the reconciliation. At the end, this medical communication service has emerged as a business on its own. At the time there have come out two larger competitors whose sole function is journal abstracting and anchor business to serving pharma. At the end, we are not competitive in this field. We were out totally.
